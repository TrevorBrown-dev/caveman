# The board cam

A live copy of the skateboard drawn in a corner of the screen, turned exactly as the board under the skater is this frame, so a stream shows how a trick flips. Trevor's ask (2026-10-22): "yank and render a copy of the skateboard in the corner... with whatever its pitch and yaw is", cloning what the board shows (the OBS feed on the deck) plus the trucks and wheels.

## How it works today (r6h, 2026-10-22)

- **Meshes**: `caveman-trainer pip load` reads the stock deck (`deck_gen_popsicle_mesh`, items.toc), trucks and wheels (`skateboard_trucks_default_mesh`, `skateboard_wheels_default_mesh`, the level root bundle) from the player's own install with caveman-core's `mesh.rs` (positions and triangles only), gives them area-weighted vertex normals turned outward by the mesh's signed volume, and sends them over the pipe (`Request::Pip { mesh }`; the DLL keeps them in `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Caveman\fx\pip-mesh.json` for its next load). Nothing of the game ships.
- **Drawing** (`overlay.rs` `pip_frame` and `draw_pip`, `shaders/pip.hlsl`): a square of the screen (`pip_size` of its height, `pip_corner`, `pip_margin`), its own depth texture (the back buffer's size, scissored to the square: a depth target must match the render target's size), a viewpoint from `pip_elevation`, `pip_azimuth`, `pip_fov`, `pip_zoom`, the board's live rotation from the renderer's board proxy (`pip_view camera` turns it relative to the game camera's heading, `world` not), a headlight from the viewpoint. The deck's underside (faces whose normal points down in the mesh's frame) samples the ridden art copy, the texture the in-world board samples (so the OBS feed plays here too), mapped flat from the deck's bounds as the game maps it (`ART_ROW_NOSE`, `ART_ROW_TAIL`, `ART_WIDEST`); the grip side, the trucks and the wheels are flat colours for now.
- **Settings**: `pip_*` keys of `bars set`; the trainer's `pip` words set them one by one (`pip elevation -30`, `pip corner tr`, ...).

## Lessons that cost a crash

- **Never drop a GPU resource a frame in flight reads.** D3D12 keeps nothing alive for a submitted command list. The cam's first builds replaced the vertex and index buffers at a mesh re-send and the old ones were freed under the previous frame's list: a page fault, `DXGI_ERROR_DEVICE_HUNG`, the game dead (14:36 and 14:55 on 2026-10-22, each right after a mesh re-send). Since r6g the buffers and the depth texture are replaced only once `Renderer::frames_done` says every frame of ours is done, and the art texture reference is kept per descriptor slot until the fence after the last frame that read it has passed.
- **A descriptor is not rewritten while a frame reads it**: the two art descriptors alternate, the other one written only once the fence shows the GPU is past its last use.
- **Allocate through `memwatch::room_for`** like everything else of ours, and expect it to refuse for the first second after an install (the memory watch has not measured yet) and for 15 s after the memory guard caught the game out of memory.

## The offline test

`tests/overlay_host.rs` `board_cam`: the host (its own D3D12 device, swap chain and Present hook, the DLL loaded dormant) makes a 512 x 2048 sRGB texture with three colour bands (nose green, middle red, tail blue), hands it to the cam as `Request::Pip { art: "0x..." }` (a test-only field the DLL refuses in the game), sends a flat slab as the deck, and reads the square back: from below the three bands show on the underside, from above the grip side is plain grey, with `pip_art` off the underside is grey too. Passing since r6h. It covers the shader, the descriptor table, the UV map and the normals test; it does not cover the game's own texture (its format) or the real meshes.

## Open (2026-10-22 evening)

- **In the game the cam is textureless** (Trevor: deck, trucks, wheels and grip all plain) while the offline test passes. Suspects, in order: the ridden art copy's D3D12 format is typeless (a shader resource view with a typeless format is invalid and samples nothing; r6g views a typeless texture as its sRGB kind and logs the format at bind: look for "board cam: the art bound" in the log), then the real mesh's normals (the signed-volume rule assumes a closed mesh). Next step if the log shows a typed format and still no art: read a pixel of the square back from the game (the host's readback, in the DLL).
- **Trucks, wheels and grip are flat colours by design** so far: the real look needs their textures (below).
- **The viewpoint**: from above you see grip tape, the art is underneath. The default is now 30 degrees below the board (`pip_elevation -30`); Trevor liked an angle from above for the flips, so the real fix is textures on both sides.

## Plan: the real look (textures on every part)

1. **UVs from the meshes.** The vertex declaration already parsed in `caveman-core/src/mesh.rs` names the UV sets (usage 33 and 34, per `knowledge/frostbite-formats.md`; formats to expect: two halves (6) or two floats (2)). Add `uvs: Vec<[f32; 2]>` to `Mesh` and eight floats a vertex on the wire (`PipMesh`), with a round-trip test on the install's three meshes.
2. **Which textures each part samples.** The mesh assets' material references (EBX) name the albedo, normal and mask textures of the deck's grip, the trucks and the wheels (the deck graphic is already known). caveman-core reads EBX for the deck build; the same reader lists a mesh's materials. Record the names in `knowledge/cosmetics.md`.
3. **Bind the game's own loaded textures.** The DLL already finds runtime textures by asset name (board.rs, the Caveman's own layers); find each part's albedo (and normal map) the same way, hold a reference, view it (typeless as its typed kind) in the cam's descriptor table (one table per part: albedo, normal, mask). No decoding or upload of ours; the textures are the game's, at the mip level it loaded.
4. **A small PBR-ish shader**: albedo x a two-light model (key from the viewpoint plus a fill), the normal map in tangent space (tangents from the UVs), roughness and metal from the mask where there is one; the deck's underside keeps the live art copy. Output encoded as now (SDR, scRGB, HDR10).
5. **Fallbacks**: a part whose textures are not found keeps its flat colour; a mesh without UVs draws as today.

Effort: 1 and 2 are a few hours with the offline test extended to a textured slab; 3 and 4 need the game for the by-name finds and a look at the result.

## The board cam window (Tauri, 2026-10-24): the direction now

Trevor's call (2026-10-23): drop the in-game drawing, which cost two GPU hangs and ran inside the game's frame, and render the board in the Caveman app instead, like the Forza companion apps: **a see-through, frameless, always-on-top window** that OBS captures as its own source ("Caveman board cam") and composes over the game. The game is only read, never drawn in.

- **The pose, from outside the game.** `caveman-trainer pose [--rate HZ]` opens Skate.exe read-only (as `probe-scene` does: `caveman_fx::scene` over ReadProcessMemory, the ReSkate-ported chain to the local skater's board entity and the camera view manager) and prints one JSON line per tick, 60 a second by default: `{"t", "camera": {pos, right, up, back, fov}, "board": {pos, right, up, forward} or null, "why"}`; `{"gone": true}` when the game exits; it also stops when its reader goes. Each tick is a handful of small reads (well under a millisecond); the worlds are rescanned twice a second until the board is found, then every two seconds.
- **The app** (`app/src-tauri/src/live.rs` `pose_start`/`pose_stop`/`attach`, `main.rs` `boardcam_open`/`boardcam_close`): the stream runs as a hidden child whose lines become `board-pose` events; the window is made with `WebviewWindowBuilder` (transparent, no decorations, always on top, no taskbar entry, no shadow) at the primary monitor's top right, loading the app's own page as `?view=boardcam&...` (`src/main.ts` mounts `components/BoardCam.svelte` for that view). The capability `default` covers both windows, with start-dragging and close for the cam.
- **The page** (`BoardCam.svelte`): the preview's deck (`lib/preview/deck.ts` geometries, grip and edge textures, trucks and wheels), the selected deck's fitted art on the bottom when asked (`art=` the build's fitted PNG, read through `read_png`), a fixed viewpoint from `elevation` and `azimuth` (degrees; below the board shows the art) and `zoom`, and the board's rotation from each frame: in the game camera's frame (`turn=camera`: the board's axes dotted with the camera's right, up and back, which is three.js camera space, so a flip looks as it does on screen) or the world's (`turn=world`). A frame of the other handedness is detected by the determinant and its z turned around; `mirror=1` swaps left and right and `nose=-1` turns the nose, for the day the live look says so. The drawn rotation eases to the newest frame (slerp 0.55 a frame). The whole window is a drag region; Escape closes it; a note in the corner says why there is no board (menu, loading, no game).
- **The Live panel** (`Live.svelte`, "Board cam window"): Open / Reopen / Close, size (px), from below or above, around, zoom, turns with the camera or the world, art, mirror, nose; options apply at the next Open. In the mock (`pnpm dev:mock`) Open is a browser popup of the same page.

**State (2026-10-24 10:45): built offline (svelte-check and cargo check clean; the page renders in the mock), not yet seen against the game.** The first live run decides the conventions: whether `turn=camera` shows a kickflip as the screen does, whether left and right need `mirror`, and which way the nose is. Not yet: the ridden board's actual art when a deck is not selected (the panel knows the picture on the board; the window takes the selected deck's), the wheels' colour, and a position and size the window remembers.

## Also open: the depth copies stop after an injection

Since about r6b the depth test's copies stop one second after every injection (the game's pass ends stop being seen by the hooks: `passes_ended` stands still, the windows log stops at 47 or 72), so the bars draw unhidden. r6h adds `hook_calls` to `draw depth status` (how often each hooked function runs at all) and `scratchpad/fx-r2/relaunch-r6g.sh` runs the A/B: counters with the cam off for 20 s, then on for 25 s.

## The replica (2026-10-30 afternoon): the game's own board in the window

Trevor's ask: "a PERFECT replica of the in game board... grip tape bump maps, textures, truck leaning properly, wheel models/textures, wheels spinning... indistinguishable (probably minus lighting)". Done by building the board from the player's install into a glTF the window loads, and posing it from the game's rig. The facts (rig order, UV layers, masks, presets, the profile) are in `knowledge/board-rig.md`; here is what the code does.

- **The model** (`caveman-core/src/boardmodel.rs`): the deck, truck and wheel meshes read whole (`mesh::read_full`: positions, both UV sets, the bone of each vertex, the normal decoded as the game's vertex program decodes the four-byte tangent space), each rigid part moved into its bone's space with the skeleton asset's `InverseModelPose` and hung under a node per run-time lane (the 16 lanes in ReSkate's order, bind transforms from the asset's `LocalPose`); the deck body split by the second UV set's UDIM tile into the wood (tile 0, the rim) and the graphic (tile 2, the bottom), the grip in tile 1; the textures decoded from the install (`texture_image`) and written as PNGs into the `.glb` with glTF PBR materials: base colour, normal, and one occlusion-roughness-metal image made from the game's masks (`orm_of`: `_msk` is R metal, G smoothness, B occlusion; a graphic's `_osk` is R occlusion, G smoothness, B metal with the ridden copy's lift of B; the grip's `_osk` only its G). The root node's `extras.caveman` carries the lane names and parents. About 30 MB, built in a second.
- **The equipped parts** (`read_board_model_for(game, &Loadout)`): the app reads the loadout from ReSkate's profile (`app/src-tauri/src/loadout.rs`, over the pure-Rust `sqlite.rs`, read only, the write-ahead log applied; the GNU toolchain here has no C compiler for SQLite itself) and the model resolves each item key through the shared bundle's item record to its preset bundle: the deck's graphic `_c` with its own or the default `_osk`/`_ny` (a Caveman deck is no game item: the Natural Blank's graphic, and the window puts the fitted art on), the grip preset's grit textures tinted by its `param_graphic_tintcolor`, the grip cutout's holes filled with the wood's top face (the two UV sets' affine map fitted on the grip mesh, 0.0002 rms), the truck preset's textures, the wheels' classic textures with `param_region_color_0` laid over them where their `_msk` B says (the urethane flat in the colour, the bearings' black seal kept; a graphic wheel's face over that where its mask says). The grip's tint goes on by putting it at the grit texture's median texel (the menu thumbnails show the preset colours themselves, not the colour times the grey texture). Anything that does not resolve falls back to the stock part and says so in `notes`.
- **The back end** (`main.rs` `board_model`): builds `%LOCALAPPDATA%\dev.caveman.app\board\skateboard-<version>-<exe size>-<exe time>-<loadout hash>.glb` once per loadout and keeps the newest six; `read_bytes` serves it to the window.
- **The window** (`BoardCam.svelte`): loads the model with `GLTFLoader`, takes the deck lane's node out of the rig into the group that gets the entity's turn (as before), and each frame sets lanes 3 to 11's nodes from the pose stream's `bones` (`pose --bones`: scale, rotation and position in the parent's frame), eased like the turn; the art goes on the material whose role is `graphic` (the fitted PNG as a texture with `flipY` off, as glTF's). It asks the back end for the model every 4 s and loads a new one when the path changes (the player equipped something else). A room environment map lights the metal and the gloss (the one thing the window does not copy from the game).
- **Checked live** (the game in a session): the window shows the game's deck with the ply edge, the checkered default trucks 00006, the pose from the rig, and whatever Trevor equips: red or orange wheels, a pink hearts grip, graphic wheels with the skate. print on their outer faces; the stock graphic deck `Real` and a star cutout resolve too (`tests/board_model.rs`). Not yet seen next to the game's board in motion: the trucks' steer and the wheels' spin direction, the normal maps' green direction, and how close the trucks' finish is (the environment map's intensity is a knob, `envMapIntensity` 0.7).
- **Trevor's first look (2026-10-30, 16:40) and what it changed.** (1) The deck art was missing: the picture on the board is now resolved in `App.svelte`'s poll (whichever page is open), by the DLL's label through the library (file name or display name) or the staged pictures (`live_staged_path`), sent as `boardcam-art` whenever the label changes and tried again while it does not resolve (the library loads after the first poll). (2) White patches flickering on the wheels: the level root's `skateboard_wheels_default_mesh` is a prototype whose UVs miss the wheel texture's face discs; the model now reads the items' own meshes, `truck_gen_default_mesh` (the bundle the truck item's model asset names) and `wheel_gen_classic_mesh` (the bundle of the wheels' base texture), which have six levels of detail and a second UV set on the wheel. (3) No screws on the grip: the wood mask's B marks the bolt heads on the top face (R metal there), and the grip shader shows the wood through; the grip bake now mixes the wood's colour and finish in where that mask says, as well as in the cutout's holes. (4) Graphic wheels: the print and its mask are laid out in the wheel's second UV set (outer face top half, inner face bottom); they are carried into the first set by rasterizing each wheel's triangles, per wheel, since the left and right wheels' outer faces fall on different discs of the shared texture; each wheel gets a material of its own. (5) Grit: the grip's normal map is pressed harder (`normalScale` 1.8) and a sparkle term is added to the grip material's shader (`gritty` in `BoardCam.svelte`: cells with random tilts that glint when they face the viewer, standing in for the game's `sparklestrength`/`sparklescale`). For looking at parts that are not equipped, `CAVEMAN_LOADOUT` (JSON with the five item keys) in the app's environment forces a loadout.
- **Not yet**: a Caveman deck's own mask and normal map (finishes) in the window (it shows the stock mask over the fitted art); stickers; the game's exact sparkle.

## Trevor's second look (2026-10-30 evening): the window reads the board itself

Trevor: "wheels look great now", but they "rotate erratically even when stationary"; the graphic still missing, with the thought that the window should "live-read whatever is on the board verbatim" rather than wait for the app to push it; the trucks' finish fine ("just differences in lighting"). Both findings were ours:

- **The twitching wheels: frames from between the game's two pose passes.** A two-second capture (`caveman-trainer pose --rate 30 --seconds 2 --bones`, scratchpad `bones-stats.mjs`) with the board standing still showed each wheel lane flipping between its own spin (163, 213, 196 and 267 degrees of spin: the four wheels had rolled to different phases) and, in 10 of 58 frames, all four at once at their bind rotation, the inverse of their truck's local quaternion from the skeleton asset (back wheels (0.3641, 0.3641, 0.6061, 0.6061), front (-0.6062, 0.6062, 0.3641, -0.3641)); the trucks' steer went to zero in the same frames. So the game writes the pose in two passes, the animation's output (no spin, no steer) and then its own procedural pass, and an outside reader lands between them about a sixth of the time. Fix (`BoardCam.svelte` `atBind`): a frame whose four wheel lanes are all within 1e-5 (cosine) of the model's bind rotations is skipped (the lanes keep their last targets); a run of such frames longer than a second (wheels that truly never spun) goes through. The reader side could retry instead; the filter needs no change in the trainer.
- **The missing graphic: a push that raced the window.** The art was emitted to the window right after `boardcam_open` returned (the page had not subscribed yet) and then only when the DLL's label changed, so a window opened late never got it (the DevTools trace of the resolution itself was clean: label `Biggie Cheese.png`, the library deck by file name, the fitted PNG). Now the window pulls, as Trevor suggested: `board_art` in the back end returns the DLL's label and the fitted PNG for it (the library by file name or display name, else the Live panel's staged pictures), the window asks at start, every 3 s and on a `boardcam-check` nudge the Live panel emits when the label it sees changes; `App.svelte` no longer reads `board status` (9 MB under the DLL's locks) every 4 s for this. The label comes from the light `status` once the game runs a DLL built from this evening on (`board::brief` carries `art`; `cargo check` passed, the DLL file is locked while the game runs, so it is built at the next chance); until then `board_art` reads the full board state at most every 12 s (or on a nudge).
- **The decal cropped (Trevor, minutes after the pull went in): the wrong UV set.** The picture was swapped onto the graphic material as a new three.js texture, and a new texture samples UV set 0 (the wood's layout, where the bottom face is the right half of a 1024-wide texture), while the GLB's graphic texture carries UV set 1 (`channel` 1, set by GLTFLoader from the material's texCoord): the window showed the right half of the picture across the deck, twice as big. `applyArt` now copies the stock texture's channel and transform onto whatever it shows (the still or the video). Checked from below and above with Trevor's Biggie Cheese deck: the whole picture along the deck, the text at one end and the cheese pendant at the other; the grip with its bolt heads on top (`replica/fix1-under-3.png`, `fix1-top-3.png`). Not yet seen running (no video was on during the session); the ring's layout is duplicated knowingly (the DLL crate is not a dependency of the app) with a unit test on the chain sizes.

Noted: the idea that the window's size is the zoom (at zoom 1 the board already fills the window's shorter side in every orientation, so resizing scales the board).

- **A freeze every two seconds** (2026-11-01, Trevor: the wheels froze in place for a moment every second or two, and now and then a whole trick). Measured with `caveman-trainer pose --rate 60 --bones --seconds 8` against the running game (scratchpad `pose-gaps.py`: a timestamp per tick and the gaps between them): the ticks came 17 ms apart with a 65-78 ms gap every 2.01 s (every 0.51 s before the board was found), which was the pose loop's own look for the game's worlds. `Runtime::worlds` opens every thread of the game (a snapshot, then `OpenThread` and `NtQueryInformationThread` for each of some two hundred) to read its TLS world pointer, and a thread shows its world only while it works in it, so the look repeats. It runs on a thread of the trainer's own now: the first look inline, then every 0.5 s until the board is found and every 2 s after, the stream merging what comes in through a channel and publishing the client context when a look arrived. The pose events are still a broadcast `emit` (only the window listens). The trainer (`target\release` and `E:\Games\ReSkate\Caveman\`) and the app are rebuilt (the app through `pnpm tauri build --no-bundle`: a plain `cargo build` made an exe that loaded the dev server URL, as `app/README.md` warns). Measured again two minutes into Trevor's next game session: 584 ticks in 9.93 s, all with the board, 17.1 ms apart at the median and 17.6 ms at the widest, no gap over 24 ms (before: 77.6 ms at the widest, a stall every 2.01 s).
