# The skater build's memory spike

Why the game freezes at a respawn, a teleport or a pause, and why it dies with "out of memory" on a machine with 64 GB: measured on 2026-10-23 with the FX DLL's allocation trace (`caveman-trainer mem trace`, `crates/caveman-fx/src/alloctrace.rs`, the reporter `tools/re/alloctrace_report.py`) and ReSkate 2.1.2's own diagnostics (`gpu_diagnostics` on, log level debug, the `perf memory` console commands).

## What happens at a skater build

- The game stages the skater's textures through **dedicated committed upload buffers**: at one build, one job thread created **380 buffers of 10 to 34 MB, 8.2 GB, inside 1.1 seconds**, all from the same call site (`Skate.exe+0x40ec43e < +0x40eb328 < +0x40e74b0`; the function at `0x40ec12f` tries a placed resource in the game's staging pool first and falls back to a committed buffer, and these are the fallback). Sizes step down by 64 or 128 KB from one buffer to the next.
- **Each buffer is written in full and copied out exactly once.** Small pieces (under about 5 MB) go through the placed pool instead and die within half a second.
- **The buffers are then held**: all 380 lived about 26 seconds and were released together when the level was torn down. In yesterday's memory watch the same pattern cleared in 4 to 9 seconds, so the hold normally lasts the build, not a copy.
- Over a whole build about 765 such buffers were made, and the "system memory for the GPU" (the upload heaps) went from 0.5 GB to 17 GB.
- Creating one of these takes 20 to 30 ms inside the runtime and driver, serialised. The render thread's own tiny allocations queue behind the burst at 25 ms each: that is the freeze.

## Why it crashes here and not elsewhere

The machine idles at about 64 GB of an 80.7 GB commit limit (browser tabs and idle agent sessions). The game commits 14 GB at rest and 31 GB at a build. ReSkate 2.1.2's memory line at 12:52:43 on the 23rd read "30.78 GB committed, 0.1 GB of the commit limit free", and three seconds later the game's allocator got null for 2 MB, printed "Failed to allocate memory. Your PC might be in a low memory situation. Please restart your PC" and wrote to address zero on purpose (`Skate.exe+0x15b59a5`). The earlier "DirectX E_OUTOFMEMORY" dialogs were the same wall hit inside the driver's allocation path. The low-VRAM crash in ReSkate's launcher notice is a different bug (a stale texture handle, fixed in 2.1.2). Trevor's choice is to fix the game's behaviour, not the machine's page file.

## The memory guard, and its limit

`memguard.rs` hooks the device's allocation calls and retries an `E_OUTOFMEMORY` for up to 45 seconds. In one respawn it rescued 13 allocations (0.3 to 3.3 s each). It cannot make memory: the device still reset 17 seconds later, and in the next session the whole PC hard-froze while the game idled at the menu (no bugcheck, no DRED fault; the board cam was still drawing, which it should not have been). Since then the guard gives up at once when the machine's commit free is under 768 MB (`COMMIT_FLOOR`), the saved overlay settings have the bars and the board cam off, and no GPU-drawing feature of ours runs in these sessions.

## What the burst is made of (the trace of 12:46)

In the one second of the burst the game made 402 placed 3D textures (8.9 GB, aliased in about 1 GB of heaps: transient volumes, R11G11B10_FLOAT around 480 x 270 x 64, shrinking a little from one to the next) and 399 dedicated upload buffers of matching sizes (8.8 GB): one staging buffer per volume, in runs of 128 with sizes stepping down from 33.8 to 10 MB in 64 or 128 KB steps. A whole build is six such runs (about 16.7 GB staged) on two job threads. Each buffer is copied out once, in full, and the game keeps every one until the build is over (26 s in that session, 4 to 9 s in a calm one), then releases them together. The copy destinations themselves are recorded by the trace since r6l.

## What the game's staging pool is (decompiled 2026-10-23 evening)

Ghidra's decompilation of `Skate.exe` (the quick project `Projects\ReSkate\ghidra\quick`, the script `ghidra\scripts\DecompileAt.java`, the C in `ghidra\quick\out`) settles what the pool and the fallback are:

- **The pool is a ring over one upload heap.** The owner's constructor (`+0x40e8250`) makes a 0x90-byte pool object at owner+0x2770 and one `CreateHeap` for it: type UPLOAD, flags 0xc0 (buffers only; 0x10c0 when the "not zeroed" setting at config+0x7d0 is on), size read from the renderer's configuration object (`[owner+0x10]`) at +0x60. The pool object: +0 the heap, +8 the ring size, +0x10 the tail, +0x18 the head, +0x20 a critical section, +0x50 a tree of the live placed buffers keyed by resource (node+0x28 holds its offset).
- **The allocator** (`+0x40ec120`, body `+0x40ec12f`) rounds the request up to 64 KB, bumps the head, keeps a buffer from straddling the wrap, and if `head + size <= tail + ring size` makes a **placed** buffer at `head mod ring size` and records it in the tree. Otherwise it makes a **committed** upload buffer (`+0x40ec43e`, our hooked site) with offset 0 and size 0 in the result, which is how the consumer later tells the two apart.
- **The tail** moves in `+0x40e9be0`: the consumer hands back a batch of {resource, offset, size} triples; each placed one is removed from the tree, and the tail becomes the smallest offset still in the tree (the head when none is left). Buffers are freed in any order, and the ring's free space is bounded by its oldest live buffer. Committed fallbacks (size 0) are skipped there and released by the consumer itself.

So the burst is the ring being too small for the build: once the build's held buffers reach the ring's size (or when one buffer alone is bigger than it), every further one is a dedicated committed buffer. **Enlarging the game's ring is therefore no fix by itself**: the tail only moves when the consumer frees, and the build's consumer frees most of its buffers at the end (the 25-second hold in the trace), so the ring would have to hold the whole build, 16 to 17 GB. It is the right finding for a report to the ReSkate developers (the size is a configuration value, read once at the renderer's construction).

Two corrections to the numbers above from a second reading of the 12:46 trace: the burst reaches 12 GB within 2 seconds (47.0 to 49.0 s) and 15 GB by 54 s, all held until the level is torn down at 73 s; and the trace's `at` field on a copy record is where in the destination the copy landed, not when, so no trace before r6r says when the copies run relative to the allocations. That timing is the one open question, and it decides the ring below.

## The fix: the staging ring (built, `crates/caveman-fx/src/stagering.rs`, r6m)

The staging buffers are used once and held for the build. A hook at that one call site can hand the game a **placed buffer in an upload heap of ours** instead of a committed one, and **reuse the slot once the single copy out of it has executed** (the copy's command list is seen at submission in the `ExecuteCommandLists` hook; a fence after it says when the GPU is done). The game keeps a valid object for 26 seconds; only the memory under it is recycled, which D3D12 allows for placed resources. A ring of 1 to 2 GB replaces 8 to 17 GB of committed upload heaps, and it also removes the 25 ms per creation. Guards: the trace already detects a second copy out of a recycled buffer (then that site falls back to dedicated buffers), the ring is only used for upload-heap buffers over 8 MB from that site, and a mapped-after-copy write by the game (unlikely, but detectable with the page-touch measure) would mean the fix is unsafe.

**The first in-game try (17:50) froze the game, and that was the ring's doing.** It learns about copies through the command-list hooks, and those were gated on the depth test, which was off for the session: the ring was blind, no slot was ever recycled, and every big staging buffer waited the full two seconds before falling back, a stall per buffer. Since r6q the list hooks follow the ring and the trace as well as the depth test (`depth::hooks_wanted`), `mem ring on` reports whether the copy hooks reach the game's lists, the wait is 300 ms, and the ring switches itself off when it is blind (four buffers handed out, no copy seen) or not keeping up (eight full fallbacks). A second lesson from the same session: with ReSkate's GPU diagnostics on, ReSkate detours whatever function a new resource's `Release` slot holds, which is our hook once installed, and a DLL another hook jumps into cannot be unmapped; the resource hooks therefore go in only for a trace, and the trainer can inject a fresh copy beside an inert one.

**The second in-game try (18:06) stuttered and the game kicked itself to the menu**, and again it was our waiting: the ring's blind guard fired after 28 buffers because the game records its copies some time after allocating, and the trace's two-second file write held the lock the job threads needed. The lesson, now a rule of the DLL: **nothing of ours ever blocks a game thread, not for a millisecond.** r6r applies it: the ring makes one attempt for a slot and otherwise lets the game's own committed buffer through at once (no wait, no blind guard, no self-off for a full ring; the second-copy guard stays), and the trace takes its lock with `try_lock` on game threads (a lost record is counted as `dropped_busy`), clones what the file lacks under the lock and formats and writes outside it. r6r also records when each first copy was recorded (`t_copy` on the copy line), so one trace answers the open question.

**State (2026-10-23 evening): r6r built and offline-tested, not yet run in the game.** `caveman-trainer mem ring on [--mb N]` switches it on (off as shipped; 1024 MB in slots of 36 MB, so 28 slots; buffers over 36 MB and under 8 MB are left alone). The host test (`tests/overlay_host.rs` `staging_ring`) pushes eight 16 MB buffers through a four-slot ring with every buffer held until the end, as the game holds them: four slots were recycled under live resources, every destination texture held exactly its own bytes, a deliberate second copy was caught and switched the ring off, and the D3D12 debug layer had nothing to say. Lock discipline matters here: the ring's lock is never held across a D3D12 call, because such a call can release an object and come back through our release hook (the first build deadlocked the host on exactly that).

**The in-game runs of 2026-10-24 morning (r6r, r6s)** settled the shape. Two traces over five builds (`alloc-trace-1792849563.jsonl`, `alloc-trace-1792849999.jsonl`; 2129 and 1532 staging buffers, 44.8 and 33.1 GB): the game fills about 380 buffers (7.8 to 8.3 GB) and then records all their copies in one go, fills the next batch and records those, and releases everything about two seconds after the build started (alloc to first copy recorded: median 0.4 s, p90 0.8 s, max 1.4 s; alloc to release: median 1.7 s). The peak of "allocated but not yet copied" is one batch, 7.9 to 8.3 GB; the peak held is both, 15 to 16.6 GB; the memory watch's system memory for the GPU peaked at 16.3 and 17.1 GB. The 1 GB ring of fixed 36 MB slots served 112 buffers and recycled its slots 84 times under live resources with no second copy, and fell back 1417 times: the shape works, the size and the slots were wrong, since a region frees only when its batch's copies have run, so the ring must hold a batch plus what the next batch stages meanwhile. The 8 GB try (r6s) measured nothing: the ring refused its own heap against the video budget (an upload heap is system memory). Also: each committed buffer cost 0.4 ms to create with 40 GB of commit free, against 25 ms the day before at the commit wall, so the day-before freezes were the machine, not the build. **r6t** is the variable-size ring: regions carved from one heap in allocation order and retired from the oldest (the game's own ring's shape, recycled on the copy's fence instead of the game's release), 10 to 12 GB for a build's 16 to 17 GB, the heap made on the worker thread (`tick`) and never in the hook, the budget check against system memory and the commit limit, and retired pointers forgotten after 10 s so an address reused by the game is not mistaken for a reread.

**The variable ring in the game (r6t, r6u; 2026-10-24 10:13, trace `alloc-trace-1792851216.jsonl`).** D3D12 refused single upload heaps of 6 GB and more (E_INVALIDARG; 2 GB went through), so the ring is made of 2 GB chunks and no region straddles one (r6u; r6t also had a race where the pipe's thread and the worker loop each made a heap, now one maker at a time). At 12 GB: seven builds in three minutes, 3471 staging buffers served from the ring, 357 let through to the game's committed path (6.5 GB in all, 1.7 GB live at most), 1141 regions recycled under live resources, no second copy. The memory watch's system memory for the GPU: 12.8 GB with the ring idle, 14.5 GB at the peak, against 17.1 GB without the ring. The shape of a build seen from the ring: a batch of about 380 buffers (8.2 GB) in half a second, all its copies recorded at once, and the next batch starting at once while the first batch's copies run on the GPU (a quarter to half a second), so the live set reaches 12.2 GB, the ring is full, and 100 to 250 buffers per build go to the game's path. **The honest gain is small:** the game's peak at a build goes from its base plus 16.5 GB to its base plus 13.7 GB, while the ring holds 12 GB all the time (`mem ring off` releases it), because the game holds its buffers only about 1.5 s longer than their copies take. The recycling itself is proven safe over 3471 buffers. What would make the gain large is backpressure: the job thread waiting, bounded to a second, for the oldest batch's fence when the ring is full, which caps the live set at one batch and the ring at about 8.5 GB (base plus 8.5 GB at the peak, 8 GB better than the game). That is a documented exception to the no-blocking rule and Trevor's call; the sane version waits only when the machine's commit is scarce (a hitch traded for the game's survival) and never when there is commit to spare. The other lever is the machine: with 40 GB of commit free this morning no build came near the wall, and the day before it was Ghidra's 21 GB and the browser that put the wall within reach.

The ring's worst case is now the game's own behaviour plus the ring's heap, so the in-game try is an experiment, not a gamble. Before it: nothing else large on the machine (the Ghidra analysis held 21 GB of commit on the 23rd, more than the game's whole burst). The protocol: inject r6r, `mem trace 180 --min 8192`, `mem ring on` (1 GB first), one far teleport or respawn, then `mem ring` and `python -I tools/re/alloctrace_report.py`. What it tells: `recycled` above zero means the copies run during the build and the ring works, and `--mb 4096` is the next size; `recycled` at zero with `fallback_full` climbing means the game runs its copies only at the end of a build, the ring cannot help, and the trace's `t_copy` minus `t` says by how much. `rereads` must stay 0. The memory watch's "system memory for the GPU" peak is the proof: 17 GB today.

## Reading a trace

```
caveman-trainer mem trace 300 --min 64      (in game, hooks only, nothing drawing)
python -I tools/re/alloctrace_report.py     (the newest file; survives a crash, appended every 2 s)
```

The report groups allocations by the game's nearest two frames with count, bytes, time per call, how many were freed and their mean lifetime, how much of each was copied out and how many pages were written, then the committed upload buffers per thread with their sizes in order, the copy destinations per site, and a quarter-second timeline against the video memory watch.
